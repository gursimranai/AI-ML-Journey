## Research Paper Reading Workflow

A useful workflow for studying a research paper is:

1. **Identify the problem:** Understand the task and why it matters.
2. **Read the abstract and introduction:** Identify the research question and claimed contributions.
3. **Study the methodology:** Understand the proposed architecture, algorithm, or training approach.
4. **Inspect experiments:** Review datasets, baselines, evaluation metrics, and results.
5. **Evaluate limitations:** Identify assumptions, weaknesses, and unanswered questions.
6. **Write a summary:** Explain the paper in your own words.
7. **Explore implementation:** When useful, examine the official code or reproduce a small experiment.
8. **Record connections:** Link the paper to related research and concepts already studied.

## Recommended Paper Metadata

When recording a paper, capture the following details where available:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Title | Full research paper title |
| Authors | Paper authors |
| Publication Year | Year of publication or preprint |
| Venue | Journal, conference, or workshop |
| DOI or arXiv ID | Persistent identifier |
| Official Link | Publisher or preprint page |
| Code Repository | Official or relevant implementation |
| Research Area | Relevant topic or category |
| Summary | Main idea and contribution |
| Limitations | Known weaknesses or open problems |

Use consistent filenames, such as `attention-is-all-you-need.md`, for individual Markdown summaries. Preserve existing filenames when they are already meaningful.

## Organization Guidelines

- Store papers in the most relevant research category.
- Keep detailed, original paper notes in `Paper-Summaries/`.
- Avoid maintaining unnecessary duplicate copies of the same paper.
- Include reliable source links and identifiers whenever possible.
- Distinguish published results from personal interpretations.
- Record related papers and follow-up research when relevant.
- Prefer official implementations and reproducible experiments when available.

## Copyright and Attribution

Respect the copyright and licensing terms of research publications. Do not upload publisher PDFs or other copyrighted material unless redistribution is permitted. Prefer linking to official publication pages, openly licensed versions, or authorized preprints.

Write summaries in your own words, cite the original authors, and clearly attribute external figures, code, datasets, and other materials.
