# Computer Vision Research Papers

This directory organizes research papers related to computer vision, enabling machines to extract meaningful information from images, videos, and other visual inputs.

It covers foundational computer vision techniques, deep learning architectures, modern vision models, visual representation learning, and emerging multimodal systems.

## Research Topics

### 1. Image Classification

Research on assigning labels or categories to images.

Topics include:
- Traditional image classification methods
- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)
- Vision Transformers (ViTs)
- Transfer learning and fine-tuning
- Self-supervised visual representation learning

### 2. Object Detection

Research on locating and classifying objects within images or video frames.

Topics include:
- Bounding-box prediction
- Two-stage and one-stage detectors
- Real-time object detection
- Anchor-based and anchor-free methods
- Detection of small and overlapping objects

### 3. Image Segmentation

Research on classifying pixels or separating meaningful regions in images.

Topics include:
- Semantic segmentation
- Instance segmentation
- Panoptic segmentation
- Medical image segmentation
- Prompt-based segmentation

### 4. Visual Transformers and Foundation Models

Research on models designed to learn general-purpose visual representations.

Topics include:
- Vision Transformer architectures
- Image-text representation learning
- Vision-language models
- Visual foundation models
- Zero-shot and few-shot recognition

### 5. Image Generation and Restoration

Research on generating, reconstructing, or improving visual content.

Topics include:
- Image super-resolution
- Image denoising and deblurring
- Image inpainting
- Generative adversarial networks
- Diffusion-based image generation

### 6. Video Understanding

Research on extracting information from video sequences.

Topics include:
- Action recognition
- Object tracking
- Video classification
- Temporal modeling
- Video-language understanding

### 7. Advanced Applications

Research related to practical and specialized computer vision tasks.

Topics include:
- Autonomous driving
- Medical imaging
- Remote sensing and satellite imagery
- Robotics and visual navigation
- Document image understanding

## Paper Reading Framework

When studying a computer vision paper, examine the following:

1. **Problem definition:** What visual task does the paper address?
2. **Input and output:** What data enters the model, and what does it predict or generate?
3. **Methodology:** Which architecture, algorithm, or training strategy is proposed?
4. **Dataset:** Which datasets are used, and how representative are they?
5. **Evaluation metrics:** Are metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, mAP, or IoU appropriate for the task?
6. **Baselines:** Which existing methods are used for comparison?
7. **Results:** What improvements are reported, and are they supported by the experiments?
8. **Limitations:** What computational, data, robustness, or generalization issues remain?
9. **Reproducibility:** Is the implementation available, and can the results be independently verified?

## Suggested Paper Summary Structure

For detailed notes, use the central [`Paper-Summaries/`](../Paper-Summaries/) directory.

A useful summary should include:

- Paper title, authors, and publication year
- Research problem and motivation
- Key contribution and novelty
- Model architecture or proposed method
- Dataset and experimental setup
- Evaluation metrics and main results
- Strengths and limitations
- Important equations or diagrams, with attribution
- Personal understanding and implementation ideas
- Links to the paper, official code, and related work

## Organization Guidelines

- Keep papers focused on visual learning and image or video understanding in this directory.
- Use meaningful filenames and preserve existing filenames when practical.
- Record the publication year, source link, and official implementation when available.
- Link detailed notes from this directory rather than duplicating the same summary.
- Distinguish published experimental results from personal observations.
- Respect copyright and publisher licensing terms; link to authorized paper versions rather than redistributing restricted PDFs.
