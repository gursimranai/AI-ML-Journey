This directory collects resources for finding research papers, tracking citations, exploring machine learning publications, accessing preprints, and evaluating research methods.

## Code and Reproducibility

Reading the paper is only one part of understanding research. Code, datasets, evaluation protocols, and implementation details help determine whether a result can be reproduced.

| Resource | Link | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub | [github.com](https://github.com/) | Locate research implementations and project repositories |
| Papers with Code | [paperswithcode.com](https://paperswithcode.com/) | Find code associated with papers and benchmark tasks |
| Hugging Face Hub | [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/) | Explore pretrained models, datasets, and demonstrations |
| Kaggle | [kaggle.com](https://www.kaggle.com/) | Explore datasets, notebooks, and applied experiments |
| Zenodo | [zenodo.org](https://zenodo.org/) | Find and cite research outputs and archived software |
| Software Heritage | [softwareheritage.org](https://www.softwareheritage.org/) | Explore a long-term archive of source code |
| OpenML | [openml.org](https://www.openml.org/) | Share datasets, machine learning experiments, and workflows |

When examining a repository linked to a paper, check whether it contains the actual implementation, training configuration, dataset instructions, evaluation scripts, and a license. A repository associated with a paper does not automatically guarantee reproducibility.

## How to Read a Research Paper

You do not need to understand every mathematical detail during your first reading. Use a structured approach.

### 1. Identify the research question

Read the title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion. Determine:

- What problem does the paper address?
- Why is the problem important?
- What limitation in previous approaches motivates the work?
- What contribution do the authors claim?

### 2. Understand the method

Study the methodology, architecture diagrams, equations, and pseudocode. Identify the input, output, assumptions, training procedure, and major components.

For an ML paper, examine the dataset, preprocessing, model architecture, loss function, optimizer, baselines, and evaluation metrics where relevant.

### 3. Inspect the experiments

Ask whether the experiments support the authors' claims.

- Are the baselines appropriate?
- Are the datasets suitable for the stated task?
- Are the metrics meaningful?
- Are ablation studies or error analyses provided?
- Are limitations and failure cases discussed?
- Are the results reproducible from the available details?

### 4. Check the limitations

A strong result on one benchmark does not guarantee performance in every real-world setting. Consider dataset bias, computational cost, generalization, data leakage, and the difference between controlled evaluations and deployment.

### 5. Record your understanding

For each important paper, write a short summary in your own words. Separate the authors' claims from your interpretation and identify questions that remain unanswered.

## Research Workflow

A repeatable research workflow helps turn paper reading into useful technical knowledge.

1. **Define a topic:** Choose a focused question, such as improving image classification with limited training data.
2. **Search the literature:** Use scholarly search engines, conference proceedings, and relevant preprint categories.
3. **Build a reading list:** Select foundational papers, recent work, and relevant surveys.
4. **Track references:** Save bibliographic metadata and notes in a reference manager.
5. **Read critically:** Identify the proposed method, evidence, assumptions, and limitations.
6. **Inspect the implementation:** Review the repository, dependencies, license, and evaluation code.
7. **Reproduce a small experiment:** Start with a baseline and follow the documented setup.
8. **Compare results:** Use consistent datasets, splits, metrics, and configurations.
9. **Document findings:** Record commands, environment versions, results, and unexpected behavior.
10. **Share responsibly:** Cite sources, respect licenses, and clearly distinguish reproduced results from your own work.

## Research Notes Template

Use the following template when documenting a paper in your repository.

```markdown

## Bibliographic Information
- Authors:
- Year:
- Venue:
- DOI or URL:
- Code Repository:

## Research Problem
What problem does the paper address?

## Main Contribution
What is new compared with previous approaches?

## Methodology
Describe the key algorithm, architecture, or mathematical idea.

## Dataset and Evaluation
- Dataset:
- Baselines:
- Metrics:
- Main results:

## Strengths
What does the paper do well?

## Limitations
What assumptions or weaknesses should be considered?

## Reproducibility
Can the experiments be reproduced from the available code and instructions?

## My Understanding
Explain the paper in your own words.

## Open Questions
What would you like to investigate next?
```
